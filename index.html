WOODEN FLATWARE

In Progress

PROCESS

Inspired by the movement of water, this

three-piece beechwood cutlery collection

explores the relationship between fluid form,

intuitive ergonomics, and functionality. The

choice of beechwood reinforces the

relationship between material and function,

allowing the natural limitations and nature of

wood to inform the purposes of the utensils

and their organic forms.

Rounded silhouettes, bulbous ends, and

continuous curves establish a cohesive visual

language while communicating the individual

purpose of each utensil. The spoon and fork

share a three-curve side profile and rounded

outer silhouette, reflecting their similar

motions during use. In contrast, the butter

knife features a two-curve profile and an

elongated, teardrop-like silhouette that

emphasizes its spreading function.

The contours of each handle intuitively guide hand placement, while flattened surfaces on the knife

provide a designated resting point for the thumb. An integrated stand elevates the food-contact

surfaces above the tabletop, promoting hygiene without compromising the collection's sculptural

continuity.

HOW MIGHT WE

PROCESS

The structure consists of an interlocking grid of cardboard panels, each with precise cuts

that allow them to slide together and hold at 90° intersections without adhesives or

fasteners. At each joint, two panels share the same slot depth, meeting at their midpoints to

form rigid intersections. This distributes load across the entire grid rather than concentrating

stress at any single point while using significantly less material than a solid or layered

construction.

By ensuring all boards were positioned with the corrugations running vertically,

compressive forces from users are transferred directly down through the flutes, rather than

across them, significantly increasing load-bearing capacity without adding material.

Fluid curves through the repetition of

stiff geometric panels are arrayed to

collectively read as a soft, organic form.
